  there is no single commercially standardized open-source hardware board or project officially trademarked under the specific name "fidel cnn tester," extensive research and interactive systems exist for Handwritten Ge'ez/Ethiopic character recognition (Fidel) utilizing Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) alongside AI/ML-driven correction pipelines.The landscape of these developments includes several notable components:1. The "Fidel" Benchmark DatasetA major breakthrough in this domain is the creation of Fidel: A Large-Scale Sentence Level Amharic OCR Dataset.It contains over 40,000 handwritten and 28,000 typed line images.It serves as a benchmark for testing seven deep learning models spanning CNN, CTC, and Transformer architectures.2. Double-Stage Recognition & CorrectionFor handwritten Ge'ez scripts, isolated character testing often fails due to the visual similarity of characters within the same base class. To implement correction mechanisms, current state-of-the-art models employ a dual-stage system:Stage 1 (CNN): A CNN architecture acts as a feature extractor to recognize individual handwritten characters.Stage 2 (LSTM/RNN): An LSTM or Connectionist Temporal Classification (CTC) network acts as the sequence modeling or "correction" layer to predict and rectify character outputs at a word or sentence level based on contextual vocabulary.3. Interactive Agents and Dashboards ("The Board")If you are referring to a digital application board or educational dashboard layout, the most prominent project is eFidel (ኢፊደል). Inspired by physical electronic talking posters, it is an AI-powered interactive digital agent that presents all 238 Fidel characters in a grid to assist in reading, recognition, and pronunciation testing through audio-visual machine learning loops.4. Implementation on Constrained HardwareFor testing on physical boards (CPUs, low RAM, or microcontrollers), open-source datasets like the Yaredoffice Ge'ez Characters Dataset on Hugging Face are explicitly designed to benchmark lightweight CNN models on constrained hardware architectures.
